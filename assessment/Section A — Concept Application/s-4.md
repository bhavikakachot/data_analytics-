## Including Restaurants with Zero Revenue

The report should use a **LEFT JOIN**, with `restaurants` as the left table and `orders` as the right table. A left join returns every row from the left table, even when no matching row exists in the right table. Therefore, restaurants with no orders are retained in the report.

```sql
SELECT
	r.restaurant_id,
	r.name,
	COALESCE(SUM(o.order_value), 0) AS total_revenue
FROM restaurants AS r
LEFT JOIN orders AS o
	ON r.restaurant_id = o.restaurant_id
GROUP BY
	r.restaurant_id,
	r.name
ORDER BY total_revenue DESC;
```

For a restaurant that has no matching order, the columns from `orders` are `NULL`. `SUM` therefore produces `NULL` for that restaurant, so `COALESCE(..., 0)` converts the result to a reported total revenue of zero.

An **INNER JOIN** would return only restaurants that have at least one matching row in `orders`. Restaurants with zero orders would have no matching pair and would be removed entirely from the result, so the report would not show them as zero-revenue restaurants. The left join is justified because the business requirement is to preserve all rows from `restaurants` and optionally attach matching order data.
